# scTMEatlas

## Systemic-to-local tumor microenvironment remodeling via glucocorticoid-responsive neutrophils across cancers
Cancer is increasingly recognized as a systemic disease, yet how systemic physiological signals modulate local tumor microenvironment (TME) remains largely unknown. We hypothesize that neutrophils are key mediators of systemic signals to the local TME. To test this, we created a neutrophil-preserving single-cell transcriptome atlas from 1,099 samples spanning 26 cancer types, establishing a unified framework for dissecting the TME diversity. From over 10 million cells, we decoded 119 robust cell subpopulations, including 19 neutrophil subsets. We resolved six recurrent TME subtypes across cancers, including a newly identified neutrophil-enriched immunosuppressive TME characterized by marked GlucoCorticoid (GC) response, termed TME4-GC. TME4-GC is significantly enriched for tumor-associated FKBP5+ neutrophils, with matched peripheral blood enriched for IL1R2+ neutrophils. Meta-program and trajectory analysis linked these two neutrophil subsets into a circulation-to-tumor GC-responsive neutrophil continuum. GC-responsive neutrophils are highly conserved between humans and mice. Functionally, GC-responsive neutrophils exhibit enhanced migration and degranulation while suppressed in pro-inflammatory chemokine production, thereby impairing T-cell infiltration and cytotoxicity. GC-responsive neutrophils also promote tumor progression in subcutaneous and orthotopic mouse models. Neutrophil-specific deletion of the GC receptor Nr3c1 attenuated stress-induced tumor progression and improved anti-PD-1 efficacy. Clinically, circulating GC-responsive neutrophils also showed potential as a noninvasive marker of TME4-GC tumors in an independent validation cohort. Together, these findings reveal a conserved systemic-to-local TME remodeling orchestrated by GC-responsive neutrophils across cancers, providing a mechanistic and potentially targetable link between host physiology and cancer immunity.
